## Intro

When I first left university at the end of 2024, I was not sure what to do with my professional life.
I was good in school, did well on my thesis and courses, and had a lot of passion for what I was studying, but I was never certain about what I *really* wanted to do in the future (it is a very long time!).
I loved studying, but I was also very intrigued with what could be done practically, and how I could apply my theoretical knowledge into a practical setting.
It was after some time of reflection and long discussions with my close circle (family and friends included) that I decided to enter the workforce in the industrial sector.

It was at that time that I got interviewed for a position for the new and exotic software innovation division at a bailiff's office in Ghent. 
I liked the idea of starting in a small team, and building everything from the ground up, and since this was a startup, I was told that we would have to research and figure many things out.

## The job

During my time at that startup I was a junior software engineer working in the backend. 
Because a text would be too long (and too boring) to read, I decided to summarize everything I did in that time with a simple bullet list.

### Projects
- **AI/LLM-powered document recognition system**: I had to research which options were the best on the market, how feasible they were, and how good they would work on the internal documents.
- **Internal chatbot to help defendants with their invoices**
- **AI-powered mail sorting system**: Integration with office mailboxes and Microsoft products to ensure correct sorting of mails for business users.
- **Integrating with third parties**: I had to make secure and auditable integrations with payment gateways, the legacy software of the bailiff's office and another legal company
- **Developing supporting libraries for internal use**: I had to write reliable, reusable and generic code to integrate AI within all our different products

The internal chatbot would later become the main business and grow to become an extensive and fully customisable AI agent-building platform, and cause the rebrand to the Interaictive company. 
In that platform, my main role was being an all-round software engineer in the backend.
It was really interesting to have been on this project since day one and seeing its growth from simple prototype to a real product to something that went beyond the scope of the bailiff's office.

### Technologies
Almost all technologies I used at the job were learned there. Some I learned (the basics) in school, but it was exciting to really learn to use them in the real world.
I learned and used all those technologies on the job to create interesting products:

- Cloud infrastructure (Azure)
- Application deployment and orchestration with Docker
- Observability and auditing with the Grafana stack
- .NET development
- API development with MCP and REST, with a focus on security
- Security with OAuth, Keycloak, OpenID Connect
- GitHub project management
- Git
- AI-assisted development
- Computer networking with firewall and DNS management
- Multithreaded and scalable programming with actor frameworks (Akka)
- Semantic Kernel (more recently Microsoft Agents) for connecting to LLM services
- Microsoft Graph API
- Telephony connection with SIP and Anynode
- Websockets and webhooks


I also got the chance to attend the CloudBrew conference in Mechelen twice to sharpen my skills.

## Conclusion
I really loved my experience there, and it taught me how to work in a startup, build production-ready software, and see first-hand how real software was built, deployed, and maintained.
Even though this job was very interesting and taught me a lot, it was not fully what I wanted to pursue as a long-term career, and since I left school I have felt the double pull of "I love research, but I also want(ed) to put that in practice and do real work."
While I was working, I kept myself up to date with research, and now that it is time to close that chapter in my career, I look forward to new challenges, and pursue something else with the knowledge and experience I gained in a real industrial setting.

I personally wish to thank Wim van Houts for being such a great manager: knowledgeable, patient, and willing to teach me everything from the ground up. His down-to-earth, no-nonsense approach was something I had never experienced before, and it helped me grow a lot, not only professionally but also personally.
